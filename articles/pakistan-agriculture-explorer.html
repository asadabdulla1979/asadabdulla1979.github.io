Assumptions and sources, in full

Wheat baseline: average yield 3.2 t/ha on ~9.25m hectares, output 29.6 million tonnes (PES 2025–26 provisional). Genetic potential ~8.0 t/ha; progressive growers achieve 5–7 (PARC; USDA; field-yield literature). Domestic consumption ~30 mt (approximate). Prices: the slider spans the actual 2025–26 range, from the post-deregulation harvest low near Rs2,500/40kg to the open-market peak of Rs4,650; Rs3,600 (~Rs90,000/tonne) is the valuation default. GDP: ~$410bn nominal. All-crops multiplier: wheat is roughly a third of major-crop value and rice, cotton, maize and sugarcane suffer comparable yield gaps; 2.5× is the conservative default. Comparators: remittances $30.3bn, goods trade gap $27.9bn, IT exports $3.4bn (FY26, SBP/PES). Farm distribution: anchored to the 7th Agriculture Census 2024 size classes (97% of farms under 12.5 acres); intermediate points are an illustrative cumulative interpolation.

Honest caveats: values are gross farmgate output, not value added; a surplus this large would depress prices and raise input bills, so the net gain to GDP is smaller than shown. The frontier is an aspiration, not a forecast. The point of the tool is the order of magnitude. Views are my own.
