The cotton we chose not to grow

This spring, Punjab planted less cotton than it did in 1966, and the world's textile workshop now imports every second bale it spins. The convenient story blames the weather. The evidence points at a guaranteed price for the thirstiest crop in the rotation, a tax that preferred foreign lint to our own, and a research system the industry stopped paying for.

The third of a harvest we never take

Pakistan grew 3.7% in FY26 and called it a recovery. Agriculture grew 2.9%, the widest miss of any sector, and the reason is not the weather. It is the gap between what our farms produce and what the same seed and soil already yield on the country's best fields, a prize worth close to a tenth of GDP, and the first reform in years to name it.
